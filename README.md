# Europe Travel Planning Notes

A practical, non-commercial planning reference for independent travelers, families and small groups visiting Europe. It covers route order, transport, seasonality and a pre-departure checklist — the decisions that make the biggest difference before you book anything.

## Start with route order, not a checklist

Most over-packed Europe itineraries fail for the same reason: the route is a list of famous places rather than a line on a map. Before booking hotels, answer three questions:

1. **Where do you arrive and leave?** Open-jaw tickets (into Paris, out of Rome) often cost little more than a return and remove a full day of backtracking.
2. **How many one-night stays?** Each hotel change consumes roughly half a day once check-in, checkout and luggage are counted. Two nights is the practical minimum; three is better.
3. **What is the one thing you will regret missing?** Build the route around that, then add stops that sit on the way.

A useful rule for a first trip: **one country per week**, or two neighbouring countries if they share a rail corridor. Three countries in ten days is usually one too many.

## Choose bases, then transport

Transport should follow the route, not the other way around.

- **High-speed rail** wins for city pairs such as Paris–Lyon, Rome–Florence–Venice, and Paris–Zurich. Book early; fares rise steeply close to departure.
- **Private car and driver** wins for rural regions, families with luggage, and routes where trains stop short: Provence, Tuscany, the Amalfi Coast, Alpine valleys.
- **Coach tours** win for maximum coverage with zero planning, at the cost of early starts and fixed schedules.
- **Domestic flights** are rarely worth it on short corridors once airport transfers are counted; use them for long legs such as Paris–Rome or Madrid–Barcelona only when time is tight.

For scenic rail in Switzerland, reserve panoramic seats early and treat the train as the attraction, not just the transfer.

## Country notes

### France

France is three or four different trips. Paris is a city break best done on foot, metro and a couple of private transfers. Provence and the Luberon reward a car and a local driver-guide; lavender peaks roughly mid-June to mid-July. The Riviera works well with trains between coastal towns, plus a car for hill villages. The Loire and Alsace are natural add-ons from Paris or Germany/Switzerland.

A comfortable first visit is **7–10 days: Paris plus one region**. Trying to add three regions usually means a trip spent in transit. See the [France destination guide](https://www.jinxieurope.com/destinations/france.html) for a Chinese-language version of these notes.

### Italy

The classic first trip is a rail spine: Rome, Florence, Venice, with Bologna and Naples as extensions. The edges — Amalfi Coast, rural Tuscany, Puglia, Sicily — are where a car and driver make the biggest difference. Book Colosseum and Vatican timed tickets weeks ahead in peak season; the Florence Duomo climb needs a separate reservation. Venice charges a day-tripper access fee on peak dates, and staying overnight changes both the experience and the cost.

**8–10 days** covers the Rome–Florence–Venice triangle at a comfortable pace. Add three to four days for the Amalfi Coast, Tuscany or the Lakes; Sicily deserves a separate week. More detail: [Italy destination guide](https://www.jinxieurope.com/destinations/italy.html).

### Switzerland

Switzerland is small, expensive and unusually easy to travel if you choose deliberately between rail and road. The Glacier Express, Bernina Express, GoldenPass and Jungfrau railway are the reason many people come; the Swiss Travel Pass covers most trains, buses and boats plus museum entry. A private car reaches Alpine villages, family-run hotels and trailheads that trains miss, but cannot compete on the classic scenic routes.

**7–10 days** covers two or three regions comfortably. Pick two bases and day-trip rather than changing hotels every night. See the [Switzerland destination guide](https://www.jinxieurope.com/destinations/switzerland.html) for seasonal and family notes.

## Seasonality and booking windows

| Season | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| April–May | Cities, countryside, fewer crowds | Variable weather; some mountain passes still closed |
| June–August | Long days, lakes, Alpine hiking | Peak prices, heat in southern cities, August closures |
| September–October | Warm light, harvest, comfortable walking | Shorter days by late October; mountain huts closing |
| November–March | Christmas markets, skiing, city museums | Short days; many rural hotels and restaurants close |

- **Peak-season hotels and scenic trains:** 4–6 months ahead.
- **Museum timed tickets:** 2–8 weeks ahead, more for the Louvre, Vatican and Colosseum.
- **High-speed rail:** as soon as the booking window opens, usually 3–4 months.
- **Private driver-guides:** 4–8 weeks is often enough, but summer and holiday weeks sell out earlier.

## Small group, private or coach?

| Style | Group size | Best for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coach tour | 35–50 | First-timers wanting broad coverage and zero planning | Early starts, limited free time |
| Small-group adventure | 12–16 | Independent travelers who still want logistics handled | Active pace, public transport, fewer hotel upgrades |
| Private tour | 2–8 typically | Families, multi-generation groups, pace control | Higher daily cost; needs more pre-trip decisions |
| Self-guided rail | Independent | Confident travelers who want flexibility | You handle bookings, luggage and disruptions |

There is no universal winner. Match the style to the travelers, not to the brochure.

## A simple 10-day first-trip outline

- **Days 1–3:** Paris — one guided museum day, one neighbourhood day, one Versailles or Montmartre day.
- **Day 4:** Paris to Switzerland by TGV; base in Lucerne or Interlaken.
- **Days 5–6:** One scenic rail leg and one lake or mountain day.
- **Day 7:** Transfer to Italy; base in Florence.
- **Days 8–9:** Florence plus a Tuscan day trip, or train to Venice.
- **Day 10:** Depart from Florence, Venice or Milan.

This is a template, not a prescription. Swap Switzerland for Provence or Italy for Spain depending on the season and the non-negotiable item on your list.

## Pre-departure checklist

- [ ] Passport valid for the period required by your destination and transit countries.
- [ ] Visa or entry authorisation checked on the official government site for each country.
- [ ] Travel insurance covering medical care, evacuation and trip interruption.
- [ ] Accommodation names and addresses saved offline for border and check-in.
- [ ] Rail passes, scenic-train reservations and museum tickets stored in one folder.
- [ ] Bank cards enabled for overseas use, plus a small cash reserve in euros or Swiss francs.
- [ ] Medication in original packaging with a copy of the prescription.
- [ ] Offline maps downloaded for each base.
- [ ] Emergency contacts and embassy details saved.
- [ ] A realistic daily budget including city taxes, meals and optional guides.

> Visa and entry rules change. Always confirm with the official embassy, consulate or border authority for your nationality and itinerary. This repository is not legal or immigration advice.
